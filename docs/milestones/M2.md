# Milestone M2 — Local Question Answering and Retrieval Improvement

**Status:** In Progress  
**Depends on:** M1 — Retrieval Foundation  
**Current completed features:** M2.1, M2.2  
**Next feature:** M2.4 — Question-Answer Evaluation and Retrieval Refinement

---

## 1. Purpose

Milestone M2 turns the reliable retrieval capability delivered by M1 into an evidence-grounded local question-answering system.

M2 shall allow a user to submit a natural-language question and receive an answer that:

- is based on evidence retrieved from the active workspace;
- preserves the provenance supplied by M1;
- identifies its supporting sources;
- distinguishes supported statements from uncertainty;
- explicitly abstains when sufficient evidence is unavailable.

M2 also strengthens the workspace, metadata, evaluation and operational contracts required for later knowledge-analysis capabilities.

---

## 2. Architectural Boundary

M1 ends with ranked retrieval.

Its contract is:

```text
Question
   │
   ▼
Retrieval
   │
   ▼
Ranked Evidence + Provenance
```

M2 consumes that evidence:

```text
Question
   │
   ▼
Existing M1 Retrieval
   │
   ▼
Ranked Evidence + Provenance
   │
   ▼
Context Construction
   │
   ▼
Question-Answer Prompt
   │
   ▼
Configured Local LLM
   │
   ▼
Grounded Answer
   │
   ├── Citations
   └── Uncertainty / Abstention
```

M2 shall not create an independent retrieval path or allow the LLM to bypass the retrieval subsystem.

The M1/M2 boundary is governed by:

```text
docs/decisions/002-m1-retrieval-boundary.md
```

## 4. M1 Retrieval Handoff Contract

M2.3 shall consume the established M1 retrieval interface.

Each item of retrieved evidence shall provide, directly or through associated metadata, at least:

- chunk identifier;
- document identifier;
- workspace;
- source filename;
- relative source path;
- source-root identity where multiple roots are configured;
- document title where available;
- section title where available;
- page or source-location information where available;
- chunk text;
- retrieval score;
- evidence metadata;
- source checksum or equivalent provenance identifier.

Optional metadata may be absent without invalidating an otherwise usable result.

Where a title, section or page is unavailable, the citation layer shall fall back to the best available stable source identity.

---

### 4.1 Ranking Contract

Retrieval results shall:

- be returned in relevance order;
- expose the effective Top-K and threshold used;
- preserve workspace and source-root isolation;
- preserve metadata filters applied to the query.

The retrieval subsystem shall document whether a higher or lower score represents greater relevance.

Similarity scores shall not be assumed to be directly comparable between unrelated queries unless the retrieval implementation explicitly guarantees this.

A valid retrieval may return zero results.

Zero results after filtering or threshold application are not an application error.

### 6.1 M2.3 Required Behaviour

M2.3 shall:

- accept a single natural-language question;
- use the existing M1 retrieval interface;
- construct bounded context from retrieved evidence;
- render the configured question-answer prompt;
- invoke the configured local Ollama LLM;
- produce an evidence-grounded answer;
- present citations derived from retrieved provenance;
- explicitly represent uncertainty;
- abstain where evidence is insufficient;
- expose sufficient diagnostics to distinguish retrieval failures from generation failures.

---

## 7. Grounding Contract

An answer is considered grounded when its substantive factual claims are supported by evidence supplied to the model for that invocation.

The application shall not treat model confidence or linguistic fluency as evidence of correctness.

Prompt instructions alone are not sufficient to define grounding behaviour.

---

### 7.1 Claim Support

Where practical, each substantive externally verifiable claim shall be supported by one or more citations.

A citation may support multiple neighbouring statements where the relationship is clear.

Introductory wording, formatting and non-factual connective text do not require individual citations.

Citations shall refer only to evidence actually supplied in the question-answer context.

---

### 7.2 Insufficient Evidence

Insufficient evidence shall be handled explicitly.

If retrieval returns no usable evidence after effective filters and thresholding, the application shall not invoke the model as though evidence were available.

It shall return an explicit insufficient-evidence result.

Where evidence exists but is incomplete, ambiguous or conflicting, the model may be invoked but shall be instructed to state that limitation.

An insufficient-evidence response is a successful application outcome and should not by itself produce a process-error exit code.

---

### 7.3 Conflicting Evidence

M2.3 does not perform formal contradiction analysis.

However, where retrieved evidence contains materially incompatible statements, the answer shall not silently choose one.

The system should:

- report the existence of differing evidence;
- cite the relevant sources;
- avoid resolving the conflict without adequate evidence;
- defer formal contradiction classification to the later contradiction-analysis milestone.

---

## 8. Citation and Provenance Contract

Every displayed citation shall resolve to evidence used in the answer context.

Minimum user-facing citation identity shall include:

- document title where available, otherwise source filename;
- section where available;
- page or source location where available;
- source-root or path disambiguation where necessary.

Absolute filesystem paths should not normally be exposed in ordinary user-facing answers.

Relative paths or stable source identifiers may be used when required for disambiguation or diagnostics.

Chunk identifiers are primarily diagnostic and need not appear in normal user output.

Documents with identical filenames in different roots must remain distinguishable.

---

## 9. Context Construction Contract

M2.3 shall define and enforce an explicit context budget.

The budget shall account for:

- system/prompt instructions;
- user question;
- retrieved evidence;
- citation/provenance material;
- output-token allowance.

The complete request shall remain within the supported context size of the configured model.

---

### 9.1 Context Selection

Evidence should normally be considered in retrieval-rank order.

The context builder should:

- remove exact duplicate evidence;
- avoid unnecessary duplication from overlapping or adjacent chunks;
- preserve provenance;
- retain whole semantic chunks where practical.

When the context budget is exceeded, lower-ranked evidence should normally be removed before higher-ranked evidence.

Whole-chunk removal is preferred to arbitrary truncation.

If truncation is unavoidable, it shall preserve sentence boundaries and provenance and shall be diagnostically visible.

---

### 9.2 Token Accounting

The M2.3 feature specification shall define the method used to estimate or calculate token consumption.

Exact tokenizer support is preferred where practical.

A conservative documented estimator is acceptable where the configured local model does not expose a convenient tokenizer.

The effective context budget and reserved output allowance shall be configurable.

---

## 10. Retrieval Override Semantics

The `ask` command should support retrieval controls consistent with `search`, including:

```text
--top-k
--threshold
--filter KEY=VALUE
```

The effective configuration precedence shall be:

```text
Command-line override
        ↓
Workspace-specific configuration
        ↓
Global/default configuration
```

CLI overrides apply only to the current invocation and shall not silently modify configuration files.

---

### 10.1 Filters

The M2.3 feature specification shall define exact filter semantics.

Unless a later approved specification defines otherwise:

- each filter uses `KEY=VALUE`;
- multiple filters are combined using logical AND;
- malformed filters are rejected before retrieval;
- unknown or unsupported filter keys produce an actionable error;
- effective filters are visible in diagnostic output.

---

### 10.2 Threshold

The feature specification shall define:

- valid threshold range;
- whether greater or smaller values indicate better matches;
- relationship to the score exposed by M1;
- behaviour when no result passes the threshold.

Threshold values shall remain configurable because retrieval scores are embedding-model and corpus dependent.

---

## 11. Answer Output Contract

Normal human-readable output should contain conceptually distinct sections:

```text
Answer

<grounded answer>

Sources

<source citations>

Uncertainty

<limitations, conflicting evidence, or insufficient evidence where applicable>
```

The exact presentation may evolve without altering the underlying data contract.

Future machine-readable output may be added independently.

---

## 12. Ollama and LLM Operational Contract

M2.3 shall define operator-visible behaviour for at least:

- Ollama unavailable;
- configured model absent;
- model request timeout;
- incompatible context size;
- malformed model response;
- empty model response;
- interrupted generation;
- other model invocation errors.

The application shall provide actionable messages rather than raw tracebacks during normal CLI use.

---

### 12.1 Exit Codes

The M2.3 specification shall define stable process exit semantics.

At minimum:

- successful grounded answer → success;
- successful insufficient-evidence response → success;
- command-line usage error → usage failure;
- configuration error → application failure;
- Ollama/model invocation failure → application failure.

Exact numeric codes shall be documented in the feature specification and CLI documentation.

---

### 12.2 Reproducibility

Model, prompt and retrieval settings used for a question shall be available to diagnostics and evaluation.

M2.4 evaluation shall pin:

- model identity/version;
- prompt identity/version;
- retrieval configuration;
- context-budget configuration;
- relevant generation parameters.

Evaluation shall not rely on undocumented defaults.

## 14. M2.4 — Question-Answer Evaluation and Retrieval Refinement

**Status:** Implemented; clean baseline and human acceptance review pending

M2.4 shall evaluate the complete question-answer pipeline without replacing the M1 retrieval evaluation contract.

M1 retrieval evaluation and M2 QA evaluation are distinct activities.

---

## 15. M1 vs M2 Evaluation Boundary

### M1 Retrieval Evaluation

Evaluates:

```text
Question
   ↓
Retrieval
   ↓
Ranked Evidence
```

Typical measures include:

- Recall@K;
- Mean Reciprocal Rank;
- retrieval latency;
- expected-source ranking.

The existing M1 evaluation capability shall remain valid and shall not silently change meaning.

---

### M2.4 QA Evaluation

Evaluates:

```text
Question
   ↓
Retrieval
   ↓
Context
   ↓
LLM
   ↓
Answer + Citations
```

It shall additionally evaluate:

- answer correctness;
- evidence grounding;
- citation correctness;
- unsupported claims;
- insufficient-evidence behaviour;
- conflicting-evidence behaviour;
- end-to-end latency.

A separate `qa-evaluate` command implements validation, execution, review
template generation, scoring, baseline designation and comparison. The existing
M1 `evaluate` command retains its retrieval-only semantics.

---

## 16. M2.4 Benchmark Contract

Before QA tuning begins, M2.4 shall define and commit a benchmark dataset.

The benchmark shall contain approximately 20–30 representative questions covering:

- direct factual questions;
- document-discovery questions;
- metadata-filtered questions;
- temporal questions;
- cross-document questions;
- evidence-based questions;
- questions with multiple valid sources;
- deliberately unanswerable questions;
- questions exposing conflicting evidence where suitable.

---

### 16.1 Benchmark Record

Each benchmark case shall support at least:

- stable case identifier;
- question;
- answerable/unanswerable classification;
- expected source document(s) or source identities;
- expected key facts or answer characteristics;
- acceptable alternative evidence;
- optional retrieval filters;
- optional notes for manual review.

Expected chunks may be recorded where stable, but document/evidence identity should not depend unnecessarily on implementation-generated chunk numbering.

---

## 17. M2.4 Metrics

M2.4 shall retain M1 retrieval metrics and add QA-specific measures.

The evaluation specification shall define calculations for:

- retrieval Recall@K;
- MRR;
- source correctness;
- citation validity;
- citation coverage;
- answer factual/key-fact correctness;
- unsupported-claim rate;
- abstention accuracy;
- answer latency;
- end-to-end latency.

Where a metric requires human judgement, a reproducible review rubric shall be provided.

---

## 18. M2.4 Completion Gates

M2.4 shall not be declared complete merely because metrics can be calculated.

Before retrieval or prompt tuning begins:

1. a benchmark version shall be committed;
2. a baseline run shall be recorded;
3. completion thresholds or regression gates shall be agreed and documented.

This prevents completion criteria from being altered after seeing the results.

At minimum, completion gates shall require:

- all displayed citations resolve to supplied evidence;
- no known regression in the M1 retrieval benchmark without explicit justification;
- acceptable handling of deliberately unanswerable questions;
- no systematic unsupported factual claims;
- reproducible evaluation under pinned model, prompt and retrieval settings.

Numerical quality thresholds shall be set in the M2.4 feature specification after the initial benchmark has been reviewed, and before optimisation work begins.

---

## 19. M2.4 Evaluation Artefacts

Each evaluation run shall produce a persistent, inspectable report containing at least:

- benchmark version;
- application version or commit;
- workspace;
- model identity;
- embedding model identity;
- prompt identity/version;
- retrieval settings;
- context settings;
- generation settings;
- per-question results;
- aggregate metrics;
- timestamps and latency information.

This allows results to be compared between changes to retrieval, prompts and models.

## 21. Explicitly Deferred Beyond M2

The following remain outside M2:

- structured knowledge extraction beyond evidence metadata;
- formal agreement/disagreement detection;
- contradiction classification;
- autonomous planning;
- agentic workflow execution;
- autonomous external actions.

These capabilities shall consume, rather than bypass, the evidence and grounding contracts established by M1 and M2.
